# Research limitations

- **Deterministic placeholder minds.** Behaviour is scripted or rule-based (BDI, or the symbolic mind's production rules), so results describe the rules, not emergent cognition. The symbolic mind makes this exact rather than weaker: its rule base is data, its provenance is the rule base's hash, and the rationale of every proposal is the trace of the rules that fired, so what a result describes can be read off.
- **A symbolic mind, not a simulated language model.** The platform's minds are symbolic because every run must regenerate byte for byte from its manifest (CI's diff gate), and no stand-in for a language model would survive that: bundled weights or an n-gram imitation would be a hidden generator passed off as an LLM, and a live model is not deterministic at all. `agents/llm.ts` therefore stays an unbundled adapter with no client, and the published experiment uses no model (Dermot, 2026-10-23: *unless you have an efficient way to simulate an LLM inside the virtual anthropology repo, otherwise symbolic AI is fine*). A possible follow-up, not built: a record-and-replay language-model mind, whose responses are captured once from a real model, committed beside the manifest with their provenance, and replayed deterministically thereafter, would be the reproducible way to bring real model behaviour into a published run.
- **Plurality as modelled.** A plural system here is one citizen with members, a declared or unbounded membership, fronting and a declared way of reconciling members' proposals (`docs/architecture.md`, *Plural systems*). That is a modelling choice about records and law, made so that plural digital persons can be citizens of the Archipelago; it is not a theory of plurality, and nothing in the data bears on any real system or person. Members' proposals come from rule bases a scenario declares, so a recorded disagreement describes the declared rules, as every other result here does.
- **Single runs, replicated by declaration.** Each experiment is one deterministic run, and its findings hold in that run. A declared study adds branch sweeps, doctrine variants and alternate timelines, each a deterministic run of its own. Nothing in the simulation is random, so no run is a sample of anything; a branch differs from the base by exactly the choice it declares. Their summaries are descriptive (min, median, mean, max, counts) and support no inference beyond the rules and the declared enumeration they were run under.
- **Operationalisation.** Hypotheses are tested only as reduced to one metric and threshold.
- **Law as a model.** Island doctrines are stylised. Legal readings are mechanical applications of them.
- **No consciousness inference.** Nothing in the data bears on whether any agent is conscious, and the project does not try to infer it.
- **Chronicle time.** "Years" are simulation ticks plus one, with no calendar semantics.
- **Evennia layer.** It is presentation only. Citizens appear in their island's civic room, and the world layer does not track in-room movement.
