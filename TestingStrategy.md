# Testing Strategy

How The Archipelago is tested, what each layer guards, and the policies that keep the gates honest. The rules the gates serve are in `CLAUDE.md`; the research methodology they enforce is in `the-archipelago/docs/research-methodology.md`.

## The governing principle

The repository's claim is that every simulation is publishable: regenerable byte-for-byte from its manifest, with no conclusion in any output that was not computed from the log. So the suite does not mainly check that the simulation produces *plausible* output. It checks that the output is **the same output** (determinism, replay, the committed golden files) and that every sentence in it **traces to something recorded** (chronicle references, findings as metric comparisons, interpretation left blank). A test that passed on a different run than the committed one would be worthless here, which is why the research tests run the real scenario against the real committed export.

## Layer 1 — the simulation suite (`the-archipelago/simulation/test/`, vitest)

Six files, 51 tests, run by `npm test` and, with the typecheck in front of it, by `npm run check`. `tsconfig.json` is strict with `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, so the typecheck is itself a gate. The suite runs on a Windows checkout too since 2026-10-23: `research.test.ts` resolves its paths with `fileURLToPath` and normalises line endings before comparing against the committed export, so an autocrlf checkout compares what CI compares.

- `kernel.test.ts` (12) — the kernel's guarantees, from a fresh *First Fork* genesis each time:
  - a choice log takes the first option by default, applies a flip at its exact (seq, citizen, label, occurrence), counts occurrences, reports moot flips and refuses an out-of-range option;
  - `World.replay` reproduces state and head hash, and tampering with one event is detected;
  - island law: Continuity prohibits copying, Fork creates separate identities without duplicating credits;
  - researchers cannot change a lifecycle state, and unregistered researchers are refused;
  - the disclosure guard requires the artificial-nature statement and blocks manipulation;
  - `EnactAmendment` is applied for the system actor `study` only (a citizen and the clock are refused at authorisation; an empty amendment is refused) and records `LawEnacted`;
  - each island's founding counting rule follows its doctrine (Continuity by the electorate, Concord by consensus, Fork and Mnemosyne by votes cast);
  - the counting rule is island law: one yes and one abstention carries by votes cast and by consensus and fails by the electorate, a tie fails under every rule, consensus needs a yes, and the closed-proposal event names the rule;
  - founding law overrides apply to statutory fields only, and a constitutional field in `laws` fails the schema;
  - every epilogue choice point is at or after the branch seq and taken by default; a timeline branched with no flips reproduces the base run and its choice log exactly; a flipped choice shares the record to its seq and diverges after; a moot flip changes nothing and is reported; an amendment at the branch is the first event after it;
  - **property** (fast-check): `irreversibly-deleted` is terminal under every transition;
  - **property** (fast-check, 25 runs): arbitrary experience and credit-transfer sequences (the generator is fast-check's, exercising the kernel; the simulation itself draws nothing) keep every invariant, conserve total supply, and replay to the same head hash.
- `enumeration.test.ts` (3) — the branch sweep's declared order: van der Corput positions; fairness across citizens, rotation of kinds, one alternative per visit in canonical order; single-option points skipped and the space exhausted cleanly.
- `symbolic.test.ts` (6) — the symbolic mind, from a fresh *First Fork* genesis each time: it proposes through the choice log and its rationale is the rule trace (the rules that fired, the facts matched, the agenda, the intention, with the tie between equally salient intentions recorded as a choice point); an open ballot fires the vote rule at salience 90 and the trace says so; outside an interlude it proposes nothing; two runs over the same world give the same proposals, trace, choice points and head hash; **property** (fast-check, 60 runs): forward chaining fires the same rules in the same order whatever order the rules are declared in, over arbitrary facts, honouring salience; a rule base is validated (unique ids, known intention kinds, at least one rule) and hashed into a `symbolic` provenance that genesis can declare per citizen; facts are strings derived from the observation, the kin choice among them.
- `plural.test.ts` (10) — plural systems as digital persons, from the *First Fork* genesis plus two plural citizens (a test fixture, not a published scenario: one on Continuity with co-fronting members of unequal say and a never-fronted member that is a subsystem of 720,000; one on Fork reconciled by concurrence): a plural system is one person in the record (one citizen id, one ledger account, one place in an electorate, one vote; members are not citizens); plurality is not copying (the system lives under Continuity's Charter, `ChangeFronting` is allowed there citing the charter rule, records `FrontingChanged` with the first-time fronts, creates no fork, keeps every state and history invariant, and replays; only the system changes its own front, only to known members, and a singular citizen has none); members are not descendants (a plural system on Fork forks into exactly the declared branches, each a plural system with the same members, and the four-descendant limit reads branches, not members); the reply opens with the artificial-nature statement, names who is fronting, passes the guard, and the guard blocks consciousness and humanity claims in the plural while admitting a statement of which member speaks and of disagreement; two members' differing proposals in one record are reconciled by `weighted-say` with the difference written into the rationale as ordinary difference, no flag and no violation; each reconciliation decides as declared, through a fronting subsystem as through a member, equal say is a recorded choice point, and fronting nobody is recorded; membership is counted or unbounded without materialising members, and the research snapshot summarises it only for plural citizens; structure is checked (unknown fronts, duplicate ids, a count below the known members) and agreement never is; **property** (fast-check, 20 runs): nested systems to depth four of any declared size parse, keep every invariant, found a world, change front and replay to the same head hash; **property** (fast-check, 60 runs): whatever two fronting members propose and whatever the reconciliation, a difference is recorded, nothing is thrown, concurrence holds back exactly on difference and weighted say follows the greater say.
- `two-voices.test.ts` (8) — runs *Two Voices in One Record* and `publishExperiment` once with a fixed clock, then asserts: the head hash and event count match the manifest's pinned values; `index.json`, `research_manifest.yaml`, `research.json`, `events.jsonl` and the scenario report are byte-identical to the committed export; the run is filed as a demonstration with symbolic minds, its four findings are consistent metric comparisons whose observed values are the computed metrics, and interpretation is left blank; the plural citizen is one of four records, changes front three times by its own command (the second a member's first front, flagged `newlyFronted`), every change cites the charter rule, no fork and no rejection occur; the reconciliations in the dataset show the members differing (the vote against the poem; later three ways) and weighted say deciding, each applied and recorded as the expected event, and the ballot holds one vote from the system; the two replies open with the artificial-nature statement and name who is fronting, the chronicle carries the change-of-front statements, and no clinical word appears in the scenario report, the timeline, the chronicle, the scenario dataset or the manifest; every chronicle statement traces to a real event with a matching hash; and two runs of the scenario give the same head hash, choice points and rationales.
- `research.test.ts` (12) — runs the scenario and `publishExperiment` once with a fixed clock, then asserts: the head hash matches the manifest's pinned value; three claimants claim continuity and the evidence returns no verdict; findings are mechanical metric comparisons; every chronicle statement traces to a real event with a matching hash; the experiment report has the standard sections and never generates interpretation; `research_manifest.yaml` carries the required provenance keys; **regeneration is byte-identical to the committed export** for `index.json`, `research_manifest.yaml`, `research.json` and `events.jsonl`; and a manifest claiming consciousness is rejected by the schema; the First Fork is filed as a demonstration and every output (index, provenance manifest, research.json, the report's abstract, design bullet and limitation) says so; and a manifest with a missing or unknown `category` is rejected while `experiment` is accepted; the study replicates the base run as declared (a branch sweep of exactly the budget in the named enumeration, its first pass giving one branch to every citizen that had an alternative, every branch completed, at or after the branch seq, distinct from the base and from each other, the `control` timeline reproducing the base head hash, the `juno-maps-instead` flip used, the single-descendant doctrine recorded `infeasible`, the no-integration doctrine completing with zero integrations, the *Study* section and its limitation present, `study:` in the provenance manifest); and a malformed study (duplicate variant ids, a zero branch budget) is rejected while a manifest without a study parses.

## Layer 2 — the Evennia bridge suite (`the-archipelago/evennia/tests/`, unittest)

12 tests, run by `python -m unittest discover -s tests -v`, needing no Evennia install: the client's request shape, refusal and id-mismatch handling and protocol-version check over a fake transport; the world plan's rooms, exits and ferry, and its rejection of duplicate room keys; formatting (the citizen description discloses artificial nature and makes no consciousness claim; `ask` parsing; citizen resolution; events by region); and two **live** tests that start the real bridge server with `npx tsx` and drive it end to end, including the refusal of an unregistered researcher. The live tests therefore need the simulation's `node_modules/` in place. They run on a Windows checkout too since 2026-10-27: `SubprocessTransport` resolves `npx` through `shutil.which`, because the bare name is `npx.cmd` there and `Popen` does not find it (the two live tests errored in `setUpClass` with *WinError 2* before).

What is not tested: the Evennia typeclasses, scripts and command set themselves, which need a running Evennia. They are thin over the tested pure modules by design (`worldplan.py`, `formatting.py`, `bridge.py`).

## Layer 3 — CI (`.github/workflows/archipelago.yml`)

On every push to `main` and every pull request, one `test` job: `npm ci`, `npm run check`, the doc check `npm run check:docs`, the site check `npm run check:site`, then **regenerate and diff** (`publish:first-fork`, `verify:first-fork`, `publish:two-voices`, `verify:two-voices`, `git diff --exit-code -- ../exports ../reports`), then the Evennia suite on Python 3.12. The Pages job publishes and verifies the same two before assembling the site. `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` is pinned at the workflow level and again in the publish script.

The diff step is the repository's golden suite. The committed `exports/` and `reports/` are behaviour-as-specification: any change that alters a simulation result fails there, and an intended change regenerates them in the same pull request. Never patch an exported file by hand to make the diff green; the `verify` step would fail on the hash anyway.

The doc check (`simulation/scripts/check-docs.ts`, added 2026-10-10 with the README-proof convention) imports nothing outside Node's standard library and is typechecked with the rest. It fails on a relative link in a README or under `docs/` that resolves to nothing, a front-matter block below the top of any Markdown file (generated reports included), and `the-archipelago/README.md` disagreeing with the disk: its directory table against the directories present, its four islands against `ISLAND_IDS`, its list of *The First Fork*'s outputs against `exports/the-first-fork-v1/reports/`. Since 2026-10-31 it also holds every test the READMEs and `docs/` cite to the suites, which is the README-proof convention made a gate: a cited test file (`kernel.test.ts`, `test_bridge.py`, with or without its path) must be in `simulation/test/` or `evennia/tests/`; a double-quoted string on a line that names a test file, or inside a parenthesis, is read as a citation and must be a substring of an `it(...)` title in a file the line names (or in any test file when the line names none, as the plural bullets do); and a backticked `SomeTests`, `SomeTests.test_x` or `test_x` must be a unittest class or method. Before that, renaming a test left its citation standing in the README with nothing behind it; 57 citations are checked today. The same run fails if `docs/evennia.md` and `evennia/README.md` differ. What it still cannot check is that the test a README cites proves the claim beside it; a change that renames a capability keeps those citations true.

The site check (`simulation/scripts/check-site.ts`, added 2026-10-27; standard library only, typechecked with the rest) is the `test` job's stand-in for the Pages job, which does not run on pull requests. It assembles the site into a temporary directory the way the Pages job does (`site/` at the root, `exports/` as `data/`, `reports/` as `reports/`) and fails if `index.html` carries an inline script, a `<style>` element, a `style=` attribute or an `on*=` handler (its CSP permits none; `docs/threat-model.md`), if a relative `src` or `href` in it resolves to nothing, if `exports/catalog.json` is missing, malformed or leaves out an export directory, if any file `app.js` fetches per dataset is absent (the list is read from `app.js` itself, with `study.json` required exactly when `index.json` declares a study), if a path in `index.json`'s `files` is missing or not the recorded size, or if the catalog's title, category, head hash or event count for a dataset disagree with its `index.json`. What it cannot check is the rendering: that each view draws what the data says.

A `pages` job runs on `main` only, after `test` passes: it publishes again, copies `site/`, `exports/` (as `data/`) and `reports/` into one artifact and deploys it. Before 2026-10-27 a break in site assembly merged green and failed on deploy; the site check now catches a missing or moved file on the pull request, and a change to the Pages job's own copy lines remains the one thing only a deploy exercises. The first run on `main` failed at `configure-pages` with *Not Found* because Pages was not enabled for the repository; the workflow passes `enablement: true` since 2026-10-01.

Everything fails; nothing warns. There is no lint and no coverage measurement yet (see gaps).

## Extending

- **A new scenario that should support timelines** implements `ScenarioDriver.branch` and attaches its epilogue minds from the record the way `attachEpilogueMinds` does, with a control-branch test proving the base head hash is reproduced.
- **A new scenario** lands with its manifest, its registry entry in `scenarios/index.ts`, the generated `exports/<id>/` and `reports/` from a pinned-clock publish run, and a `research.test.ts`-style test that runs it and compares against the committed export. From then on CI's diff holds it still.
- **A new invariant** goes in `kernel/invariants.ts` and gets a fast-check property in `kernel.test.ts` that drives arbitrary command sequences through it, the way supply conservation and replay equality are pinned now.
- **A new policy guard** (a disclosure pattern, an authorisation rule, an island-law clause) lands with both verdicts: the case refused and the nearest case admitted.
- **A new mind** implements `CitizenMind`, draws every option through the choice log, and lands with a determinism test (two runs, same proposals and head hash) and a test reading its rationale; a symbolic rule base lands with a test naming the rule it expects to fire and the trace it expects to read.
- **A change to plurality** (a reconciliation, a membership shape, a command on a system) keeps two things pinned: that a plural system is one citizen and never a copy or a fork under any island's law, and that no test, invariant or guard turns on members agreeing.
- **A new metric** is defined in `research/metric-registry.ts`, so it is reproduced in every report and manifest, and used by a hypothesis whose finding the research test can check as a mechanical comparison.
- **A new chronicle or report statement** must cite an event; the traceability test will fail if it does not, which is the intended way to find out.
- **An intentional output change** regenerates the exports in the same change and says in the pull request what moved.

## Known gaps (candidates for next)

- **No coverage measurement.** `coverage/` is already gitignored, but no coverage provider is installed and CI runs none. The account's convention is a ratchet at the measured baseline, raised only with the tests that earn it; measure first, then add the gate at the number measured.
- **No lint.** Neither ESLint nor Biome for the TypeScript, nor ruff for the Python. Add one green on its first run, never red; a permanently failing check is the one everyone learns to ignore.
- **Studies summarise, they do not infer.** Branch sweeps, doctrine variants and timelines exist now, but their summaries are min, median, mean, max and counts; no test checks that a report's language stays descriptive beyond the printed limitation, because that is a judgement. The next step on the research side, if wanted, is a declared comparison between groups with its own operationalisation, never a generated one.
- **The site's rendering is untested.** `check:site` (2026-10-27) holds the files and the catalog still; nothing yet loads `app.js` against the exports and checks that each view draws what the data says. A headless-browser smoke test, or a Node test that runs the pure parts of `app.js` (the Markdown renderer, the layouts) over the committed exports, would be the next step, and it is a judgement whether the site has earned that.
- **Actions pinned to Node 20 majors.** The runner warns that `checkout@v4`, `setup-node@v4` and `configure-pages@v5` target the deprecated Node 20; bump them when their next majors are stable.
- **Two identical docs** (closed 2026-10-31). `docs/evennia.md` and `evennia/README.md` are byte-identical and `check:docs` now fails when they differ; collapsing one into a pointer remains an option.
- **Counts in the prose.** `CLAUDE.md` and this file say "51 tests", "12 tests" and give a count per test file; nothing holds those numbers to the suites the way the README's citations now are. A count check in `check-docs.ts` is the next cheapest addition, if the numbers prove to drift.
